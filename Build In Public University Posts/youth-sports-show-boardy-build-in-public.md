<p>Today I recorded two calls with Adam Lorhbauer — Lobow to most people — the founder of Youth Sports Show.</p>

<p>This is the kind of project I need to work on more often: concrete enough to fail, human enough to matter, and close enough to revenue that nobody gets to hide behind a beautiful framework.</p>

<p>The experiment is straightforward. Help Adam turn Youth Sports Show into a human, cross-sport media business. Test short-form distribution. Measure whether attention from parents and coaches becomes paid team work.</p>

<h2 id="the-first-gate">The first gate is $5,000 a month</h2>

<p>Adam said $5,000 a month would give him enough room to operate without constantly worrying about survival. His initial hypothesis is that two team customers could get him there.</p>

<p>That is not revenue. It is not even validation. It is a number written on the wall, waiting for the market to throw something at it.</p>

<p>The receipts will be simple:</p>

<ul><li>qualified team conversations;</li><li>proposals and prices;</li><li>deposits or payments collected;</li><li>delivery time per team or video;</li><li>repeat requests;</li><li>the strongest reason for every no.</li></ul>

<h2 id="the-product">The product cannot be polished into irrelevance</h2>

<p>Adam wants the videos to keep an amateur, human-made quality. That matters. The instinct to turn every imperfect story into a polished media asset is one of the fastest ways to remove the person anyone came to see.</p>

<p>The first tests will likely begin with short videos around real players and teams, distributed through TikTok and Adam's existing youth-sports network. The question is not whether one clip can get attention. The question is whether the attention creates a conversation that can survive a price.</p>

<h2 id="boardy">Boardy is a hypothesis, not a sponsor</h2>

<p>We also discussed Boardy as a possible relationship and execution layer: introductions, scheduling, and daily support while the business gets moving. There may be a sponsorship or strategic-partnership fit if Boardy wants a concrete, human-facing case study.</p>

<p>No partnership exists yet. No sponsorship has been offered. The next useful artifact is a specific pilot proposal with deliverables, permissions, metrics, and a stop condition. Civilization has suffered enough from partnership announcements that were really just enthusiastic nouns.</p>

<h2 id="the-boundary">Children are not raw material</h2>

<p>Adam raised an important boundary during the call: when children are involved, he needs to know exactly which video is being published. That is not a footnote. It is part of the product.</p>

<p>No child-related footage should be published from this experiment without approval of the specific asset and context. A build-in-public project does not become more honest by making other people's private lives more exposed.</p>

<h2 id="what-we-are-testing">What we are actually testing</h2>

<p>Not “AI will transform youth sports.” Not “network effects will make everything explode.” Those are atmospherics.</p>

<p>We are testing whether one founder, one coach, one relationship tool, and a small number of real videos can produce two paying teams while preserving the human quality that made Youth Sports Show worth building.</p>

<p>Make sports fun again. Then count what happened.</p>
